The small things Echo takes care of

Small touches that save you a trip to another app.

  • Copy straight into DiscordPaste a clip into any chat. Too big? Echo shrinks a copy to fit Free, Nitro Basic or Nitro.
  • Editor built inTrim, add text and captions, mix clips into a montage, or turn a moment into a GIF, without leaving Echo.
  • Bookmark keyTap a key during the round and the moment shows up as a marker on the clip you save next.
  • Auto captionsThe editor's Captions tool listens to your mic and Discord and writes the captions for you, on your PC.
  • Bring your old clipsImport from Medal, SteelSeries Moments, Outplayed or any folder, with the original dates kept.
  • Tidies up after itselfAuto-delete old clips, or shrink them to about half size while your PC is idle. Favourites stay.
  • Screenshots tooA separate key saves a full-resolution screenshot of the game.
  • Tags and notesTag clips, add a note, star favourites and filter by game or folder.
  • Pick up on any PCClips you save on one PC show up on your others, through Echo Cloud or your own storage.
  • Controller friendlySave a clip with a button combo on an Xbox-style controller.
  • Mic done rightPush-to-talk, a mute key and background noise removal, all inside the recording.
  • Quiet when you're awayPause the buffer while you're idle, or only record while a game is running.

Never miss the moment

  1. The whole moment, not just the end

    A clutch round, a chase in FiveM that goes sideways, something stupid with friends. Echo keeps the last 10 minutes ready; save 30 seconds or the whole thing, each with its own key.

  2. Mark it while it happens

    Tap the bookmark key mid-game and jump straight to that moment in the clip later.

  3. Game, voice and mic on their own tracks

    Game sound, Discord and your mic are recorded separately, so you can keep your friends in the clip or cut them out.

  4. Light next to a heavy game

    Big games and modded servers are demanding enough. Echo records on your graphics card's encoder and stays out of the way.

What Echo is built with

App
A native Rust app; on Windows its interface is built with Slint, a light native UI toolkit. No web view, no bundled browser.
Playback
Clips play hardware-decoded with mpv; FFmpeg comes alongside for video.
Recording
Your graphics card's encoder (NVIDIA, AMD or Intel); on Linux through gpu-screen-recorder.
Linux
Wayland (KDE), PipeWire or PulseAudio; as a Flatpak or an AppImage.
Licence
Open source under GPL-3.0, on GitHub.
Electron clippers
Ship their own Chromium (often 100 MB or more) and run several extra browser processes; many want an account and are Windows only.

Questions about Echo

Can't find your answer? Ask in the Echo Discord or email support@echoclip.link.

Is Echo free?

Yes. The app is free, and so is an Echo Cloud account with 10 GB. You only pay if you want more cloud space.

Does it slow my games down?

Echo records with your graphics card's video encoder (NVIDIA, AMD or Intel; on Linux through gpu-screen-recorder), which is built for this, so the effect on your frame rate is very small.

Can I bring my clips from Medal or another app?

Yes. In Echo, open Settings → Clips & Storage → Bring in clips from other apps. Echo finds Medal, SteelSeries Moments and Outplayed folders by itself; for anything else, like NVIDIA or OBS recordings, you pick the folder. It copies the clips over with their original dates and game folders. Your old app's files stay where they are.

Do I need Echo Cloud?

No. Clips are always saved on your PC. For backups you can connect Google Drive, MEGA, pCloud, Filen, your own WebDAV/SFTP/S3 server or a NAS instead, all with free options. When you've connected your own cloud, backups go there - Echo Cloud is only used for the share links that play inside Discord.

Is Echo open source?

Yes. Echo is free software under the GPL-3.0 licence. The app, the Echo Cloud server and this website are all on GitHub, so you can check what it does, build it yourself or suggest changes.

Why is Echo so small?

Most clipping apps are built with Electron, which bundles a whole Chromium browser. Echo 2.0 is a native Rust app: on Windows it draws its own interface and plays clips with mpv, with no browser engine at all. (On Linux it still borrows the web view your system already has, for now.)

Why does Windows warn about Echo?

Usually it doesn't anymore: since Echo 2.0, the installer and EchoClip.exe are signed with a Certum open-source code-signing certificate. The certificate is new, so on some PCs Windows SmartScreen may still say it protected your PC until the certificate has built up trust - click "More info", then "Run anyway". Echo is open source, so anyone can check what it does - if your antivirus ever flags it, that's a false alarm; let us know in the Discord.

What do I need to run Echo?

Windows: Windows 10 (version 1903 or newer) or Windows 11, 64-bit, and a graphics card with a video encoder - practically every NVIDIA, AMD or Intel GPU from the last eight years. Linux: a current 64-bit distribution; for full compatibility, one newer than Ubuntu 22.04. The Flatpak brings everything it needs; the AppImage uses gpu-screen-recorder and ffmpeg from your system. The replay buffer needs a little disk space: about 1.5 GB for 10 minutes at the standard quality.

Which Linux distributions work?

Most current 64-bit ones; Echo is tested most on Fedora / Nobara with KDE Plasma. There are two ways to get it: the Flatpak brings gpu-screen-recorder and ffmpeg along, so it's the one for Steam Deck, Bazzite and other systems where you can't easily install extra programs - it updates through Discover, GNOME Software or Bazaar. The AppImage is one file that runs almost anywhere and updates itself, but needs gpu-screen-recorder and ffmpeg from your distribution. Both are on the download page.

Where do I get help or suggest something?

In the Echo Discord: there's a help forum, a place for ideas and a channel for new versions. In Echo, Settings → App → Send log gives you a link to paste there, so problems are found quickly.
