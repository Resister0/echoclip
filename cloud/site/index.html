Features

Everything after the moment, handled

No recording button to remember. Echo keeps the last few minutes ready, so you only save the parts worth keeping.

Instant replay

Press your save key (or a controller button) and the last minutes are saved. Uses your graphics card, so games keep running smoothly.

Trim, caption, done

Cut the clip, add text and captions, mix several clips into one and export. Echo can even write captions from what was said.

Clean audio

Game sound and your mic on separate tracks, push-to-talk, and background noise removed from your microphone.

Links that play in Discord

Share a link and it plays right in the chat. Or copy the clip itself, automatically shrunk to fit Discord's upload limit.

Your clips, everywhere

Back up to Echo Cloud, Google Drive or your own storage, and get clips from your other PCs. Free up disk space without losing anything.

Knows your games

Clips are named and sorted by game automatically. Record only while a game is running, and pause when you're away.

Whatever you play

Every game gets its own folder, named with date and time, so the clip you want - or need for a report - is quick to find.

Straight into Discord

Copy a share link and it plays right in the channel - no upload limit. Or paste the video itself, shrunk to fit.

Light by design

Built to stay out of your game's way

A clipping app runs the whole time you play, so it should be small. Echo is written in Rust and uses the web view your system already has, instead of shipping a full browser of its own.

Echo

  • Rust core: the app itself is about 12 MB (FFmpeg comes alongside for video)
  • Uses the system's own web view (WebView2 on Windows, WebKitGTK on Linux)
  • Records with your graphics card's encoder (NVIDIA or AMD)
  • No account, ads or overlay needed to save a clip

Typical Electron-based clipper

  • Ships its own copy of Chromium, often 100 MB or more
  • Several extra browser processes running in the background
  • Often wants an account before you can clip
  • Usually Windows only

Setup

Up and running in a minute

The setup asks four quick questions. After that Echo just runs in the tray.

Install Echo

Download it above and start it. Windows may say it protected your PC - click More info → Run anyway. (Echo is new, so it isn't signed yet.)

Pick a save key

Choose the key or controller button that saves a clip, your microphone and the quality.

Play

Something great happens? Press the key. A small popup confirms the clip is saved.

Share it

Open Echo, trim the clip and copy a link or the video straight into Discord.

Questions

Can't find your answer? Ask in the Echo Discord or email support@echoclip.link.

Is Echo free?

Yes. The app is free, and so is an Echo Cloud account with 10 GB. You only pay if you want more cloud space.

Does it slow my games down?

Echo records with your graphics card's video encoder (NVIDIA or AMD), which is built for this, so the effect on your frame rate is very small.

Do I need Echo Cloud?

No. Clips are always saved on your PC. For backups you can connect Google Drive, MEGA, pCloud, Filen, your own WebDAV/SFTP/S3 server or a NAS instead, all with free options. Only the share links that play inside Discord need Echo Cloud.

Why does Windows warn about Echo?

Echo isn't code-signed yet, so Windows SmartScreen doesn't recognise it. Click "More info" and then "Run anyway". Signing is on the way. Echo is open source, so anyone can check what it does - if your antivirus ever flags it, that's a false alarm; let us know in the Discord.

What do I need to run Echo?

Windows: Windows 10 (version 1903 or newer) or Windows 11, 64-bit, and a graphics card with a video encoder - practically every NVIDIA, AMD or Intel GPU from the last eight years. Linux: a current distribution with gpu-screen-recorder and ffmpeg installed. The replay buffer needs a little disk space: about 1.5 GB for 10 minutes at the standard quality.

Where do I get help or suggest something?

In the Echo Discord: there's a help forum, a place for ideas and a channel for new versions. In Echo, Settings → App → Send log gives you a link to paste there, so problems are found quickly.

Is Echo open source?

Yes. Echo is free software under the GPL-3.0 licence. The app, the Echo Cloud server and this website are all on GitHub, so you can check what it does, build it yourself or suggest changes.

Why is Echo so small?

Most clipping apps are built with Electron, which bundles a whole Chromium browser. Echo is built in Rust with Tauri and borrows the web view your system already has, so the app itself is only about 12 MB.

Which Linux distributions work?

Most current ones, tested on Fedora / Nobara with KDE. Echo also needs gpu-screen-recorder and ffmpeg from your distribution.
